# Four Epistemic Orientations (FEO)

> FEO traces how phenomena are approached through sequence, configuration, mechanism and essence—and how movement among these orientations shapes discovery, learning and cross-domain knowledge transfer.

## Overview

Four Epistemic Orientations (FEO) is a developing framework for examining how something first becomes available for observation and inquiry, how it is transformed into other forms of understanding, and what changes when it travels between domains.

The orientations are not kinds of knowledge or conventional logical systems. They describe different starting accesses to a phenomenon:

| Orientation       | Guiding question                                                            |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sequence**      | How does it unfold, develop or arrive?                                      |
| **Configuration** | How is it arranged through positions, relations and surrounding conditions? |
| **Mechanism**     | How is it produced, operated or maintained?                                 |
| **Essence**       | What is it taken to be, and through what identity or defining distinction?  |

A pattern, profile or collection of relations is not automatically a configuration. Configuration requires the arrangement itself to become an object of understanding.

## Core proposition

Inquiry rarely receives a phenomenon in a complete form. It begins through access to one or more orientations and may need to develop the others.

For example:

* repeated events may be converted into an identity;
* an identity may guide the search for a mechanism;
* a mechanism may produce a sequence of observable effects;
* a configuration may explain why the same mechanism behaves differently across contexts.

These movements are not necessarily equal or reversible. A conversion can preserve some features while suppressing, adding or substituting others.

## Conversion dynamics

FEO studies:

* where inquiry begins;
* which orientations are available or inaccessible;
* how one orientation is converted into another;
* what is preserved, added or left behind;
* whether a conversion is direct, composite or substituted;
* how easily the resulting object can be reopened;
* how domain boundaries, limited effort and institutional arrangements affect its travel.

FEO therefore concerns both the production of knowledge and its movement through learning, interdisciplinary inquiry and transdisciplinary practice.

## Current application: mathematical tools

The first developed case examines how mathematical tools become institutional authority objects.

A mathematical output does not carry complete institutional authority by itself. In some use-paths, its conditional mathematical identity is replaced by an institutional identity—such as “high risk,” “ineligible” or “priority”—while its mathematical precision continues to supply credibility.

An institutional threshold-producing action then gives the output decisional privilege. Once incorporated into records and procedures, the institution’s own sequential operations may preserve and reinforce the substituted identity.

This case forms an interface between FEO and **Jurisdictional Recession (JR)**:

* FEO identifies the movement between mathematical and institutional identities.
* JR explains how the activated identity acquires jurisdiction and why access to its production, decision and reversal points recedes.

## Status

FEO is an independent working framework under active development. Its four orientations and basic conversion architecture are sufficiently stable for application, while questions about conversion types, contextual asymmetry, reversibility and transdisciplinary use remain open.

The case studies should be read as conceptual tests rather than completed empirical validation.
